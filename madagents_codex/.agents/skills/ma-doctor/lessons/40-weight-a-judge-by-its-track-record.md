# 40 — Weight a judge by its track record

> Weight a contributor by demonstrated correct outcomes in the specific category — not by activity, volume, or a uniform prior. A page or judge earns standing by having produced a right answer, not by being long or frequently touched.

Ensemble systems aggregate with weights dynamically adjusted by each member's historical accuracy in
a category — whichever predicts more accurately *for that category* gets more say. Reputation-weighted
platforms (where historically accurate forecasters get higher weight) beat egalitarian, money- or
volume-weighted ones on deep-expertise questions. The unifying rule: prioritize demonstrated
directional edge over activity — consistent profit growth is the real evidence of judgment, not
high volume.

**In a harness like this.** This is what the matured memory and wiki encode — which consultant and
which wiki page has produced correct, source-grounded answers in which slice. It is the consumer-side
rule for memory and wiki content: a page earns standing by having produced a right answer, not by
being long or frequently touched. Pairs with lesson 20's standing-cost thread (a page that never pays
off is dead weight) and with lesson 39 (a judge is weighted within its domain, not across). The
dynamic, per-category weighting is the memory and wiki maturing with use.

Adapt it to the stable-source lens on the consumer side: a MadGraph wiki page's standing is
**source-grounding** (a cited file:line is binary-correct, superseded if contradicted — lesson 28's
self-correction), not a probabilistic Brier-weight — for deterministic source, "weight by track
record" becomes "verified against source," and the probabilistic per-category weighting the lesson
describes (a forecasting-judge mechanism) is largely not applicable. The producer side is where the
gap usually lives: when learned content is frozen into a shipped snapshot, weight or gate what gets
promoted by whether it has demonstrably produced right answers, rather than shipping every page a
session happened to write. Learning sessions vary widely in how good their output is, so promoting
any of them with no quality gate ships a judge that was never weighted by its track record (the
producer-side counterpart of lesson 24's curation point).

## Where the books say it

- **Polymarket** — §07/§09/§05. §07's ensemble bots aggregate with weights dynamically adjusted by historical Brier score, so whichever model predicts a category more accurately gets more say; §09's reputation-weighted platforms (Metaculus, O.LAB) outperform money-weighted ones on deep-expertise questions; §05 insists on prioritising profit leaders over volume leaders, since consistent profit growth is the real evidence of judgment.
