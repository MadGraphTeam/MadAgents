# 32 — Surface a choice well

> Give the user a reason, not just a prompt. When you offer a choice: 3-4 materially-distinct options, each with an honest weakness and a real cost. And aggregate questions — don't spam.

A good surfaced choice gives information for a decision, not just a yes/no button: a crisp
risk/why line the user can act on. When it is a real choice, the options pattern is concrete —
3-4 options (2 too few, 5+ is decision fatigue), genuinely different at the *type* level (not
reworded variants), each with at least one explicit weakness, and real effort/cost estimates so
the user can weigh ideal against feasible. And bubble up requests: batch uncertainties into one
surfacing rather than firing N dialogs.

**In a harness like this.** When the lead surfaces a choice or a hedge, attach a short *why* the
user can act on, and when it offers options, make them a small set of materially-distinct,
honestly-costed alternatives — not three phrasings of one answer. The lead aggregates consultant
uncertainties into one surfacing, not many. This is the concrete authoring craft behind
surface-don't-decide (lesson 30).

## Where the books say it

- **Skills** — ch9. The Options pattern: three or four is the sweet spot; the differences must be genuine and at the type level; every option carries at least one explicit weakness; include real-world constraint and cost assessments.
- **Src-Analysis** — ch5 (give users what they need to decide, not just buttons — a reason, not just a prompt), ch9 (permission bubbling — aggregate questions, don't spam).
