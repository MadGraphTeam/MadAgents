# 41 — Record the thesis, not just the verdict

> A bare conclusion with no reasoning attached is unusable downstream — nobody can tell when it applies or when it has gone stale. Record the basis, the why, and the conditions alongside the verdict.

If you see only the single signal that someone acted on — the verdict, not the thesis behind it —
you cannot judge when to act on it, when it stops applying, or whether they have already changed
their mind. A recorded answer without its reasoning is a copy-trade-without-thesis: confidently
followed, silently misapplied. The cure is to store the *why* and the conditions with the conclusion,
so the next reader can decide whether it transfers.

**In a harness like this.** A wiki page or memory lesson that records only the answer ("use flag X")
without the basis (the source location, the why, the conditions) is a copy-trade-without-thesis — the
next consultant cannot judge whether it transfers to a different question. The cure is the discipline
to enforce on every learned page: cite the source, state the basis, record the conditions, so the
next reader can decide whether it applies. Pairs with lesson 17 (a labeled return) and lesson 25 (the
derived layer must trace to source).
