# 30 — Keep the human on the loop

> Execution can be outsourced; deciding can't. Surface directional choices and keep the human the decider — an advisor, not a boss.

The costs of removing the human all flow from the human *leaving*: verification debt (output piles
up unchecked), comprehension rot (the map in your head stalls), cognitive surrender (you stop
having an opinion and take whatever it hands back). The defense is to keep deciding: "the loop can
execute for you; it can't decide for you." The shape is input → proposal → human decides →
execute → human confirms — the agent is an advisor, the human keeps final authority. Notably,
even a book written for an autonomous, bot-dominated arena lands here: use AI as a research
assistant, not a fund manager.

**In a harness like this.** The system keeps the human on the loop, so those costs invert into
design justifications: staying communicative, surfacing choices, preserving the hedge the user
acts on, and keeping the user the decider are exactly what spare the user verification debt and
cognitive surrender. Most autonomy-oriented book advice has to be re-weighted *toward*
surface-don't-decide for an interactive, human-present setting — this principle argues for
human-on-the-loop directly.

## Where the books say it

- **Loop** — §07/§08/§06. The four costs of the human leaving; execution can be outsourced but deciding cannot; the human did not leave so much as change desks.
- **CC-Guide** — §09b. "On Dependency" — the subtlest pitfall is the user over-trusting and surrendering judgment; surface directional choices to keep them engaged.
- **Hermes** — §05/§21. Fowler's in/on/out-of-loop; however clever the reins become, the hand holding them had better still be yours.
- **Skills** — ch7. Humans stay present at every critical juncture and the skill advises rather than commands — amplify, don't replace.
- **Polymarket** — §07/§10. Keep decision-making authority yourself and let the model be a research assistant rather than a fund manager; the signal is valuable even unacted-on.
