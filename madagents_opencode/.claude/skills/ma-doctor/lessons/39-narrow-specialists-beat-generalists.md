# 39 — Narrow specialists beat generalists

> A narrow specialist that owns one slice beats a generalist asked to do everything. Domain edge does not transfer — outside its slice, a specialist's reliability collapses to baseline.

The empirical, money-grounded finding: the top performers are almost all *domain* specialists, and
when one ventures outside its domain its performance is no better than random. Information advantage
is highly domain-specific and does not carry across. The discipline that follows is Buffett's circle
of competence — knowing what you don't know matters more than knowing what you do — so a
contributor stays in its lane and an orchestrator routes rather than answers outside its competence.

**In a harness like this.** A roster of narrow specialist consultants, each owning one slice of the
domain (here, the MadGraph stack), beats one generalist asked to do everything. "Edge does not
transfer" *is* the lane discipline — a consultant must not answer outside its slice, where its
reliability collapses to baseline — and the lead, forbidden from answering domain claims itself, is
circle-of-competence applied to the orchestrator: it knows what it does not know and routes. The
on-the-loop user gets the specialist's depth without the generalist's confident-shallow error.
(Distinct from lesson 13, which is about the *context-isolation* value of a subagent, not the value
of specialization itself.)
