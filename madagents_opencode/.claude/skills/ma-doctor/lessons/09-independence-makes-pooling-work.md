# 09 — Independence makes pooling work

> Pooled judgments cancel error only when they are *independent*. Correlated agents share a blind spot that survives the vote.

The wisdom of crowds rests on four conditions — diversity, independence, decentralization, an
aggregation mechanism — and the load-bearing one is independence: "when enough *independent*
judgments are pooled together, random errors cancel each other out." The counter-example is the
expert herd: reputational pressure makes experts converge on the safe consensus, so their
judgments correlate and the pooling stops working. The fix for verification is the same — give
each checker a *distinct* lens, not a rerun of the same one.

## In a harness like this

Pooling consultant or reviewer outputs cancels error only when those outputs are genuinely
independent. If every consultant shares the same pretrained prior and walks the source the same
way, their agreement is *correlation, not corroboration*, and a shared blind spot survives the
vote. This is the mechanism behind distinct-lens verification: separate lenses (physics / math /
numerics) are a team that argues, not an echo chamber.

## Where the books say it

- **Polymarket** — §01. The four conditions, with independence load-bearing; an expert herd converges on the safe consensus, correlating their judgments and breaking the pooling.
- **Loop** — §07. One person plus a pile of loops becomes an echo chamber where nobody argues, unlike a team that reviews and argues with each other; a real check needs different instructions, ideally a different model.
- **Hermes** — §17. P3 voting runs N *independent* agents and an aggregator picks or merges — far more reliable than a single agent grinding out one version.
